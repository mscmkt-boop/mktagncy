How to Structure a Local Landing Page for Multi-Location Brands

By Legwork Editorial Team · February 9, 2026

  • local SEO
  • multi-location
  • landing pages

Multi-location brands consistently run into the same problem: location pages built from one template, with only the address and phone number changed, end up competing with each other for the same local searches instead of each ranking for its own market.

The structural fixes that matter most

  • Unique, locally specific content. Local manager quotes, service-area nuance, and location-specific inventory or offering details, not templated boilerplate.
  • Per-location schema. Each page should carry its own LocalBusiness schema with accurate address, hours, and geo-coordinates, not a single sitewide block reused everywhere.
  • Location-specific reviews. Pulling reviews tied to the correct Google Business Profile location, rather than a pooled sitewide review feed.
  • Clean internal linking. A locator page linking to every location, and each location page linking back to relevant category or service pages, so the whole structure reinforces itself instead of relying on one page to do everything.

The failure mode to avoid

The most common mistake is treating location pages as a programmatic SEO exercise: generate one template, swap in city and ZIP data, and publish hundreds of near-identical pages. Search engines increasingly recognize and suppress this pattern, and it undermines the map pack visibility it’s meant to build. Frequently asked questions

How much unique content does each location page need?+

There's no fixed word count, but each page needs genuine local specificity — staff, service-area detail, local reviews, location-specific schema — beyond the city name and address. Pages that differ only in a swapped city name are the most common cause of location pages competing with each other instead of their intended local competitors.
