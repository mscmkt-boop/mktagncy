Most GA4 properties we audit undercount organic search's actual contribution, usually for the same handful of configuration reasons rather than any limitation of GA4 itself.

## The most common culprits

- **Cross-domain tracking gaps**, especially between a marketing site and a separate booking or checkout domain, which break the session and misattribute the conversion
- **Default channel grouping issues**, where paid or referral traffic gets miscategorized as organic (or the reverse) due to missing UTM parameters or misconfigured referral exclusions
- **Conversion events that don't map to real business outcomes** — tracking a "page view" as a conversion instead of a qualified form submission or completed purchase
- **Data retention and sampling settings** left at defaults, which quietly limit how far back reliable reporting can go

## What accurate attribution unlocks

Once tracking is fixed, SEO reporting can move past traffic and rankings to the numbers a marketing director or founder actually cares about: organic-driven leads, revenue, and conversion rate by landing page type. That's the baseline every other report in an SEO program should be built on.
